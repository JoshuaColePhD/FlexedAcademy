## Daily class structure (50-minute periods — do not deviate)

| Time | Segment |
|---|---|
| 5 min | Do Now — prompt on board; students work independently on entry |
| 10–15 min | Direct Instruction — teacher models, explains, annotates |
| 15 min | Group Work — collaborative task tied to the day's skill |
| 15 min | Individual Work — independent task; submitted or collected |
| ~5 min | Buffer / transitions / wrap-up |

## Weekly rhythm — standard week

| Day | Personality | What happens |
|---|---|---|
| **Monday** | Anchor Text Introduction | Introduce the week's text/concept. Do Now = cold-read reaction. Group = first-read discussion or annotation. Individual = written first impressions. |
| **Tuesday** | Skill Deep Dive | Model the week's analytical skill (DIDLS, SPACE CAT, claims, etc.). Group = collaborative annotation. Individual = skill-application paragraph. |
| **Wednesday** | Application Day | Apply Tuesday's skill to a new text. Individual work alternates: odd weeks = independent analysis; even weeks = MCQ practice. |
| **Thursday** | Written Assessment | Timed written assessment — alternates every week (see below). |
| **Friday** | Reflection & Journal | Always includes Columnist Journal entry. Mode follows Thursday type. |

### Thursday alternating pattern (week number drives this)

- **Week A (odd week numbers):** Skeleton Essay — thesis, outline, topic sentences only.
- **Week B (even week numbers):** Full Timed Essay — complete draft under AP conditions.

### Friday mode (follows Thursday)

- **After Week A (skeleton):** Writing day — expand skeleton into full draft or at least one body paragraph.
- **After Week B (full essay):** Peer review + AP rubric scoring + written reflection.

### Project weeks

Some weeks replace the standard rhythm with a larger project (project brief, student
checklist, rubric, timeline). Flag explicitly when planning; do not apply the daily
structure to project weeks.

## Analytical frameworks (used all year)

- **SPACE CAT** — Speaker, Purpose, Audience, Context, Exigence, Choices, Appeals, Tone
- **DIDLS** — Diction, Imagery, Details, Language, Syntax
- **REHG** — Reason, Evidence, Historical example, General example
- **1-4-1** — Thesis paragraph (1) + Body paragraphs (4) + Conclusion (1)

## Essay rubric (1–6 progressive scale)

| Score | Requirement |
|---|---|
| 1 | Defensible thesis (real claim, not a prompt restatement) |
| 2 | Clear topic sentence in the first body paragraph |
| 3 | Specific evidence supporting that topic sentence |
| 4 | Substantial commentary — HOW the evidence supports the claim |
| 5 | Second body paragraph also achieving levels 2–4 |
| 6 | Nuanced thinking, complexity, or stylistic maturity |

## Recurring assignments

- **Columnist Journal** — students follow a self-selected columnist (headlinespot.com)
  all year; each entry: SPACE CAT analysis + DIDLS analysis + rhetorical pre-write.
  Entry #1 due end of Week 1 (Friday); subsequent entries due every Friday.
- **Dialectical Journals** — style-focused notes (diction, tone, imagery); comment on
  intended effects, not just identification.
- **Daily Notes & Analysis** — reader-response and investigative writing every class.
